ABOUT / THE PRODUCT

A homeowner should not have to know which department owns which rule before they can understand what a project involves.

THE PROBLEM

Home projects cross boundaries.

A basement renovation can involve building, fire, electrical, plumbing, energy, and egress questions. An addition can bring zoning, setbacks, lot coverage, trees, conservation, historic review, engineering, and inspections into the same project.

Home Project Planner brings those pieces together around one address and one defined project. It uses official property information to establish context, then asks only questions that can change the planning path.

The output is deliberately practical: requirements grouped by certainty, official sources attached to the relevant work, preparation guidance, and a project sequence that can be revisited as the work progresses.

It is not the City, an approval system, or a substitute for a designer, contractor, architect, engineer, inspector, or other qualified professional. It is a planning layer that makes fragmented information easier to navigate before a homeowner spends time or money on the wrong next step.

WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

Less searching. More knowing what to do next.

For a homeowner, the hard part is often not finding a single permit page. It is understanding the relationships between zoning, building, trade permits, site conditions, historic or conservation review, inspections, and closeout.

Home Project Planner is designed around those relationships. The system should be able to say what is established, what is conditional, and what still needs confirmation—then put those findings into a practical order.

The standard is evidence-backed planning. Official government information should remain the source of truth. Where the available information cannot answer a property-specific question, the planner should surface that uncertainty rather than manufacture certainty.

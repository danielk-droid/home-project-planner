# Do I need a permit for a pergola or freestanding arbor in Newton, MA?

> The official sources reviewed do not establish one universal permit exemption or size threshold for every pergola, arbor, or trellis. A permanent structure should be checked with Newton ISD and Planning for its classification, size, height, foundation, and location. Historic district exterior-work rules may also apply.

## Building and zoning review

- The state building code regulates construction of buildings and structures, while Newton zoning contains rules for accessory buildings and other structures. (Source: [Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, Tenth Edition, Section 105.2](https://www.mass.gov/doc/bbrs-10th-edition-building-code/download); [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Section 3.4.3](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638055970231470000))

## Historic review

- Exterior changes to properties in Newton's Local Historic Districts require a Historic District Application. (Source: [Newton Historic Preservation, Submit an Application](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/divisions/historic-preservation/submit-an-application))

## Questions and answers

### Is every open-roof pergola exempt because it has no solid roof?

The official sources reviewed do not establish that rule. Ask Newton how the specific design is classified before relying on an exemption. (Source: [Inspectional Services Department](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/inspectional-services))

## What to confirm with the City

- Whether the proposed design is considered a building, accessory structure, or landscape feature
- Whether a building permit is required based on size, height, and construction
- Applicable zoning setbacks
- Whether a foundation or permanent anchoring changes the review
- Historic, tree, wetlands, and stormwater requirements
- Contact: [Inspectional Services Department](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/inspectional-services)

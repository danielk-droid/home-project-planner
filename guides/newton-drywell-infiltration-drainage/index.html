DRYWELLS

When is an on-site drywell or stormwater infiltration system required in Newton, MA?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 2 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Newton's stormwater ordinance regulates certain land-disturbance and new-impervious-area projects, but the sources reviewed do not establish one universal drywell size or a citywide rule requiring every homeowner to install a drywell. Whether an infiltration system is needed depends on the project, site conditions, and Newton's stormwater requirements.

Official source: Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion Control Ordinance ↗ Newton Engineering Division ↗

Stormwater permit triggers

  • Newton's stormwater ordinance identifies land disturbance over 5,000 square feet and certain residential projects creating 401 to 1,000 square feet of new impervious area as permit categories. (Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion ↗)

Engineering review

  • Newton Engineering reviews site plans involving new drainage and expansions of impervious area. (Newton Engineering Division ↗)

Questions and answers

Is a drywell always required for a new roof or driveway?
The official sources reviewed do not establish that universal rule. Ask Engineering whether the project requires infiltration, a stormwater plan, or another approved drainage approach. Source: Newton Engineering Division ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Whether the project triggers Newton's stormwater permit thresholds
  • The required design storm and runoff volume used for sizing
  • Whether soil testing or groundwater separation information is required
  • The drywell location, overflow route, and maintenance requirements
  • Whether wetland, floodplain, or utility constraints affect infiltration

Contact: Newton Engineering Division ↗

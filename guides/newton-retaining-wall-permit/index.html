RETAINING WALLS

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall in Newton, MA?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 4 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

A retaining wall no higher than 4 feet, measured from the bottom of its footing to the top of the wall, may qualify for the state building-permit exemption if it does not support a surcharge or impound certain liquids. Newton zoning separately requires a special permit for a retaining wall 4 feet or higher, so a wall can trigger local zoning review even when it falls within the state building-code exemption.

Official source: Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, Tenth Edition, Section 105.2 ↗ Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Section 3.4.3 ↗ Newton Engineering Division ↗

State building-code exemption

  • The state code exempts qualifying retaining walls no higher than 4 feet when height is measured from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall. The exemption does not apply to walls supporting a surcharge or impounding the specified classes of liquids. (Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 C ↗)

Newton zoning review

  • Newton zoning requires a special permit for a retaining wall 4 feet or higher. The ordinance also addresses combined walls located within 25 feet of each other. (Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Sec ↗)
  • Newton Engineering reviews site plans involving retaining walls and grade alterations. (Newton Engineering Division ↗)

Questions and answers

Can a 4-foot retaining wall need a Newton special permit even if the state building code exempts it from a building permit?
Yes. The state building-code exemption and Newton's local special-permit rule are separate requirements. Source: Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, Tenth Edition, Section 105.2 ↗, Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Section 3.4.3 ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • How the wall height will be measured under each applicable rule
  • Whether nearby walls must be measured as a combined wall
  • Whether the wall supports a surcharge or affects drainage
  • Whether Engineering requires a site plan or other technical information
  • Whether wetlands, easements, or historic review apply

Contact: Planning & Development Department ↗

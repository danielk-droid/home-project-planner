# How does Newton zoning define and measure a half-story?

> Newton defines a half-story as a story directly under a sloping roof where the area with a ceiling height of at least 7 feet is less than two-thirds of the area of the story below. This zoning definition should be applied to the actual floor plan and roof geometry.

## The half-story test

- Newton's definition uses a ceiling-height threshold of 7 feet and compares the qualifying area with the area of the story directly below. (Source: [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638887019852300000))

## Project implications

- A dormer or attic conversion should be checked against the half-story definition as well as the property's separate height and floor-area limits. (Source: [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638887019852300000))

## Questions and answers

### Is a ceiling height of exactly 7 feet included in the calculation?

Yes. Newton's definition uses an area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or greater. (Source: [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638887019852300000))

## What to confirm with the City

- The measured floor area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or greater
- The area of the story directly below
- Whether the proposed roof form changes the calculation
- Whether the property is subject to an overlay district or separate height provision
- Whether the project requires a building permit or zoning relief
- Contact: [Planning & Development Department](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning)

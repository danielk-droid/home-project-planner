# How do wetlands protection laws affect home projects in Newton, MA?

> Newton requires Conservation Commission review for land-altering work within 100 feet of a vegetated wetland, intermittent stream, or pond; within 200 feet of a perennial stream; or in a FEMA or City floodplain. The state Wetlands Protection Act also regulates alteration of protected resource areas. A permit may be required before work begins.

## Newton review areas

- Newton identifies work within 100 feet of a vegetated wetland, intermittent stream, or pond and within 200 feet of a perennial stream as requiring Conservation Commission review. (Source: [Newton Wetlands Permitting Information](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/divisions/conservation-office/wetlands-permitting-rev))

## State Wetlands Protection Act

- Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 131, Section 40 regulates removal, filling, dredging, or alteration of specified wetlands and other protected resource areas. (Source: [Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 131, Section 40, Wetlands Protection Act](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIX/Chapter131/Section40))

## Questions and answers

### Does wetlands review apply only when a homeowner builds directly in a wetland?

No. Newton identifies review areas extending beyond the resource itself, including the 100-foot and 200-foot areas listed in its wetlands guidance. (Source: [Newton Wetlands Permitting Information](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/divisions/conservation-office/wetlands-permitting-rev))

## What to confirm with the City

- Whether the property contains or is near a regulated resource area
- The exact resource-area boundary and applicable review distance
- Whether the proposed work is exempt or requires an RDA or NOI
- Whether FEMA or City floodplain rules also apply
- Whether tree, stormwater, engineering, or building permits are separately required
- Contact: [Newton Conservation Commission](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/divisions/conservation-office)

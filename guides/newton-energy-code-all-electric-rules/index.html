ENERGY CODE

Do new homes and renovations in Newton have to be all-electric?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 3 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Yes, for projects that trigger the rules. Newton says complete building permit applications received after January 1, 2024 fall under the Specialized Energy Code. Starting January 1, 2025, all new construction and major renovation projects must electrify heating, cooling, cooking and clothes drying.

Official source: Energy Code / Fossil Fuel-Free ↗

What Newton says

  • Complete building permit applications received after January 1, 2024 are subject to the Specialized Code. (Newton energy code ↗)
  • Since January 1, 2025, all new construction and major renovation projects must electrify heating, cooling, cooking and clothes drying, with electricity or thermal solar for hot water. (Newton energy code ↗)
  • Newton's 2026 notice says projects that trigger the fossil-fuel ordinance must be fully all-electric. (Newton energy code ↗)
  • Electrical permit applications are filed online. (Electrical permits ↗)

Questions and answers

Does Newton require all-electric homes?
For new construction and major renovations, Newton requires electrified heating, cooling, cooking and clothes drying (since January 1, 2025). Source: Energy Code / Fossil Fuel-Free ↗
What energy code applies in Newton?
The Specialized Code applies to complete building permit applications received after January 1, 2024. Source: Energy Code / Fossil Fuel-Free ↗
Do I need a permit to install a heat pump?
Heat pumps involve electrical work, and Newton electrical permits are filed online by a licensed electrician. Confirm any other permits with the City. Source: Electrical Permits ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Whether your renovation counts as a 'major renovation' under the ordinance.
  • Which permits a heat pump installation needs.

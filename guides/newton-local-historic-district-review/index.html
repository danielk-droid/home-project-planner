HISTORIC

What needs approval in a Newton local historic district?

SHORT ANSWER

In a Newton local historic district, exterior changes, including hardscaping, need review by the Historic District Commission. Visible alterations need a Certificate of Appropriateness. In-kind work, changes that can't be seen, and excluded work can get a Certificate of Non-Applicability, and in-kind repairs may qualify for administrative approval.

Official source: Historic Preservation ↗ Auburndale Historic District Commission ↗

What the City says

  • Exterior changes in a local historic district, including hardscaping, require HDC review. (Historic Preservation ↗)
  • A Certificate of Appropriateness covers visible alterations the commission finds appropriate. (HDC FAQ ↗)
  • A Certificate of Non-Applicability covers in-kind work, changes that can't be seen, or excluded work. (HDC FAQ ↗)
  • Exterior building changes require an application, and in-kind repairs may qualify for administrative approval. (HDC FAQ ↗)

Questions and answers

Do I need approval to replace windows in a historic district?
Exterior building changes need an application. In-kind work may qualify for a Certificate of Non-Applicability or administrative approval. Source: Auburndale Historic District Commission ↗
Does a patio or walkway need review?
The City says exterior changes, including hardscaping, need HDC review. Source: Historic Preservation ↗
What is a Certificate of Appropriateness?
Approval for visible alterations that the commission finds appropriate. Source: Auburndale Historic District Commission ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Whether your property is inside a local historic district.
  • Which certificate your project needs.

HALF-STORIES

How does Newton zoning define and measure a half-story?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 2 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Newton defines a half-story as a story directly under a sloping roof where the area with a ceiling height of at least 7 feet is less than two-thirds of the area of the story below. This zoning definition should be applied to the actual floor plan and roof geometry.

Official source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions ↗

The half-story test

  • Newton's definition uses a ceiling-height threshold of 7 feet and compares the qualifying area with the area of the story directly below. (Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Def ↗)

Project implications

  • A dormer or attic conversion should be checked against the half-story definition as well as the property's separate height and floor-area limits. (Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Def ↗)

Questions and answers

Is a ceiling height of exactly 7 feet included in the calculation?
Yes. Newton's definition uses an area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or greater. Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • The measured floor area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or greater
  • The area of the story directly below
  • Whether the proposed roof form changes the calculation
  • Whether the property is subject to an overlay district or separate height provision
  • Whether the project requires a building permit or zoning relief

Contact: Planning & Development Department ↗

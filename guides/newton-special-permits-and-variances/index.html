ZONING RELIEF

What is the difference between a special permit and a variance in Newton?

SHORT ANSWER

The City Council grants special permits, by a two-thirds vote of all councilors, together with site plan approval. Variances are heard by the Zoning Board of Appeals. A variance requires conditions such as soil, shape or topography that especially affect the property, plus substantial hardship.

Official source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30 ↗

Special permits

  • The City Council grants special permits by a two-thirds vote of all the Council (Sec. 7.3.3.A). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • A special-permit application requires concurrent site plan approval (Sec. 7.3.1.A). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • Findings include an appropriate site, no adverse effect on the neighborhood, and no nuisance or serious vehicle or pedestrian hazard (Sec. 7.3.3.C). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • Examples include increased FAR and a third story for detached houses (Sec. 3.1.9.A.2, 3.2.11.A.2). (Zoning Ord. ↗)

Variances

  • Variance applications are heard by the Zoning Board of Appeals (Sec. 7.6.1.A). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • The property must have soil, shape or topography conditions that especially affect it, rather than the district generally (Sec. 7.6.1.B). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • Literal enforcement must cause substantial hardship (Sec. 7.6.1.C). (Zoning Ord. ↗)
  • Relief must not substantially harm the public good or undermine the ordinance's intent (Sec. 7.6.1.D). (Zoning Ord. ↗)

Questions and answers

Who approves special permits in Newton?
The City Council, by a two-thirds vote of all councilors (Sec. 7.3.3.A). Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30 ↗
Who hears variances?
The Zoning Board of Appeals (Sec. 7.6.1.A). Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30 ↗
Can I get a special permit to build a larger house?
The ordinance lists increased FAR as something a special permit may allow (Sec. 3.1.9.A.2). Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30 ↗
What do I need to show for a variance?
Conditions of soil, shape or topography that especially affect the property, substantial hardship, and no substantial detriment to the public good (Sec. 7.6.1). Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30 ↗

ADU SETBACKS

What does Massachusetts law say about municipal setbacks for ADUs?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 4 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Massachusetts law protects a single ADU in a single-family residential zoning district from being prohibited or unreasonably restricted, but it expressly allows reasonable local rules for dimensional setbacks, bulk, height, and site-plan review. ADUs therefore do not receive a blanket exemption from local zoning dimensions. Newton's current ADU ordinance must be checked for the particular unit.

Official source: Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 40A, Section 3 ↗ 760 CMR 71.00 — Protected Use ADUs ↗ Newton Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU) ↗

What state law protects

  • Massachusetts prohibits municipalities from unreasonably restricting or requiring discretionary zoning approval for the single protected ADU described in Chapter 40A, Section 3. (Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 40A, ↗)

What local rules may still regulate

  • The statute permits reasonable regulations concerning dimensional setbacks, bulk, height, and site-plan review. (Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 40A, ↗)
  • Newton publishes local ADU size, height, and setback standards for detached units. (Newton ADU page ↗)

Questions and answers

Does state ADU law eliminate Newton's setback rules?
No. State law expressly allows reasonable local dimensional setback, bulk, and height regulations. Source: Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 40A, Section 3 ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Whether the ADU qualifies for the state protected-use provision
  • The Newton zoning district and the applicable ADU setback rule
  • Whether the unit is internal, attached, or detached
  • Whether historic, wetlands, floodplain, or other site rules apply
  • Whether the property is subject to a prior ADU approval or transition provision

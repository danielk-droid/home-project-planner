# Do I need a permit for a deck?

Usually, yes, for a new deck, a rebuilt deck, or a deck attached to the house. Reviewers care most about structure, height, stairs and guards, and where the deck sits on the lot.

## What makes a deck a permit project

- Height above grade. Raised decks need guards and stairs that meet code, and a structure sized for the load.

- Attachment to the house. A ledger connection to the house framing is a structural detail that reviewers check closely.

- Footings. Footing size and depth depend on the design and the site.

- Location. A deck can count toward setbacks or lot coverage under local zoning, and it can affect trees or nearby wetlands.

Repairing a few boards is different from replacing the structure. If you are unsure which your project is, describe the actual work to the building department rather than guessing.

## Before you ask for quotes

- Approximate deck height at its highest point.

- Whether it attaches to the house or changes structural parts of the house.

- Whether you are adding stairs, guards, lighting or outlets. Electrical work is a separate permit.

- Distance to the property lines, and whether any trees are nearby.

## How deck projects are handled in Newton, Massachusetts

- Deck height, structure, stairs and guards feed into the building-code review under the [Massachusetts State Building Code — 780 CMR](https://www.mass.gov/massachusetts-state-building-code-780-cmr). Exact guard, stair, structural and foundation requirements depend on the final design. Newton's [Building Permit Application Checklist](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29379/638630352968235564) lists the application documents.

- Building work goes through Newton's building-permit workflow in the [Newton NewGov Permitting Portal](https://newtonma.viewpointcloud.com/).

- Exterior construction triggers a [Tree Permit](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/parks-recreation-culture/urban-forestry/tree-preservation-ordinance) evaluation even if no tree is removed.

- If the property is in a local historic district, exterior changes follow the [Historic District review](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/historic-preservation) track.

- Mapped wetlands, streams or floodplain nearby can route the site to [Conservation review](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/planning/divisions/conservation-office/wetlands-permitting-rev).

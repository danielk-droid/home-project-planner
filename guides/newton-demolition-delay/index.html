What is Newton's demolition delay, and does it apply to my house?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 2 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Full or partial demolition of any building at least 50 years old, including garages and sheds, needs Newton Historical Commission review. If the Commission finds the building Preferably Preserved, demolition is delayed 12 months, or 18 months for National Register-listed properties.

Official source: Newton Historical Commission ↗

What triggers review

  • Demolition and partial demolition of buildings at least 50 years old, including garages and sheds, require review. (Historical Commission ↗)
  • Partial demolition includes removing, altering or covering at least half of any single elevation or roof surface. (Historic Preservation ↗)

The delay

  • A Preferably Preserved finding triggers demolition delay. (Historical Commission ↗)
  • The delay is 12 months for properties not on the National Register, and 18 months for National Register-listed properties. (Historical Commission ↗)
  • A full demolition waiver cannot be granted until at least four months after the Preferably Preserved finding, and it requires Commission approval. (Historical Commission ↗)

Questions and answers

Does demolition review apply to my garage or shed?
Yes, if it is at least 50 years old. Source: Newton Historical Commission ↗
What counts as partial demolition?
Removing, altering or covering at least half of any single elevation or roof surface. Source: Historic Preservation ↗
How long is the demolition delay?
12 months, or 18 months for National Register-listed properties. Source: Newton Historical Commission ↗
Can the delay be waived?
A full waiver needs Commission approval and cannot be granted until at least four months after the Preferably Preserved finding. Source: Newton Historical Commission ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Your building's age and National Register status.
  • Whether your scope counts as partial demolition.

Contact: Historic Preservation ↗

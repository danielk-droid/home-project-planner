OPEN PERMITS

How do I close an open permit in Newton, MA?

SHORT ANSWER

Old permits do not close automatically. The completed work has to be inspected. Close the electrical, plumbing, gas and other trade permits first, then request the final building inspection. Only the owner or the contractor of record should call.

Official source: How to Close Open Permits ↗

What the City says

  • Old permits do not close automatically; completed work must be inspected. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Check the complete list of open permits. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Find your district inspector using the GIS browser linked on the City's closure page. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Close electrical, plumbing, gas and other department permits before requesting the final building inspection. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Only the property owner or contractor of record should call. Inspectors will not schedule closure inspections for brokers, agents, or new buyers. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Current work gets scheduling priority over old projects. (Closing permits ↗)
  • Don't pay a project in full until the final inspection and the permit is closed in the system. (Closing permits ↗)

Questions and answers

Do old permits expire or close on their own?
No. The City says they do not close automatically. The completed work must be inspected. Source: How to Close Open Permits ↗
Can my real estate agent schedule the closing inspection?
No. The City says inspectors will not schedule closure inspections for brokers, agents, or new buyers. Source: How to Close Open Permits ↗
In what order do permits close?
Trade permits (electrical, plumbing, gas and others) close before the final building inspection. Source: How to Close Open Permits ↗
When should I pay my contractor in full?
The City advises waiting until after the final inspection and closure in the system. Source: How to Close Open Permits ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Which permits are open on your property, and who the district inspector is.

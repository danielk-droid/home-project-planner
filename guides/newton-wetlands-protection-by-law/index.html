WETLANDS

How do wetlands protection laws affect home projects in Newton, MA?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 3 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Newton requires Conservation Commission review for land-altering work within 100 feet of a vegetated wetland, intermittent stream, or pond; within 200 feet of a perennial stream; or in a FEMA or City floodplain. The state Wetlands Protection Act also regulates alteration of protected resource areas. A permit may be required before work begins.

Official source: Newton Wetlands Permitting Information ↗ Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 131, Section 40, Wetlands Protection Act ↗

Newton review areas

  • Newton identifies work within 100 feet of a vegetated wetland, intermittent stream, or pond and within 200 feet of a perennial stream as requiring Conservation Commission review. (Newton Wetlands Permitting Information ↗)

State Wetlands Protection Act

  • Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 131, Section 40 regulates removal, filling, dredging, or alteration of specified wetlands and other protected resource areas. (Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 131, ↗)

Questions and answers

Does wetlands review apply only when a homeowner builds directly in a wetland?
No. Newton identifies review areas extending beyond the resource itself, including the 100-foot and 200-foot areas listed in its wetlands guidance. Source: Newton Wetlands Permitting Information ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • Whether the property contains or is near a regulated resource area
  • The exact resource-area boundary and applicable review distance
  • Whether the proposed work is exempt or requires an RDA or NOI
  • Whether FEMA or City floodplain rules also apply
  • Whether tree, stormwater, engineering, or building permits are separately required

Contact: Newton Conservation Commission ↗

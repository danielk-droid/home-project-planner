DORMERS

What are the zoning and permit limits for roof dormers in Newton, MA?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 3 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

A dormer that changes a roof or building structure should be reviewed by Newton ISD and checked against the property's zoning rules. Newton defines a half-story using the amount of floor area with a ceiling height of at least 7 feet under a sloping roof. The official sources reviewed do not establish one universal dormer-width limit for all Newton houses.

Official source: Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, Tenth Edition, Section 105.2 ↗ Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions ↗

Building permit

  • The state building code requires a permit for regulated construction and alteration of a building. (Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 C ↗)

Half-story treatment

  • Newton's half-story definition uses the area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or greater under a sloping roof compared with the floor area of the story below. (Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Def ↗)

Questions and answers

Does a dormer automatically make an attic a full story?
Not automatically. The zoning definition depends on the qualifying ceiling-height area and the area of the story below. The design must be measured against the current ordinance. Source: Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • The applicable dormer-width and roof-form rules for the zoning district
  • Whether the dormer changes the half-story calculation
  • Whether the dormer affects building height or a required setback
  • Whether the work changes the home's floor-area ratio
  • Whether historic review applies

Contact: Planning & Development Department ↗

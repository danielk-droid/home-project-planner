STORMWATER

When does a residential project trigger a Newton Stormwater Management Permit?

Updated November 13, 2026 · Built from 2 official City of Newton and Massachusetts sources, each linked below.

SHORT ANSWER

Newton's stormwater ordinance requires a permit before certain land-disturbance projects that could disturb more than 5,000 square feet. It also identifies residential projects creating 401 to 1,000 square feet of new impervious area as a minor stormwater-review category. Other triggers include certain retaining walls and trench excavation requiring dewatering.

Official source: Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion Control Ordinance ↗

Land disturbance threshold

  • Newton's ordinance lists projects that will or could disturb more than 5,000 square feet of land as requiring a stormwater management and erosion control permit. (Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion ↗)

New impervious area

  • The ordinance lists residential development or redevelopment creating 401 to 1,000 square feet of new impervious area as a minor stormwater management category. (Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion ↗)

Questions and answers

Can a small residential project trigger stormwater review even when it disturbs less than 5,000 square feet?
Yes. The ordinance separately lists certain residential projects creating 401 to 1,000 square feet of new impervious area as a minor stormwater category. Source: Newton Stormwater Management and Erosion Control Ordinance ↗

What to confirm with the City

  • The total land-disturbance area
  • The net new impervious area
  • Whether the project is a residential development with four or fewer units
  • Whether a retaining wall or dewatering trench triggers review
  • Whether an exemption under Newton's stormwater rules applies
  • Required calculations, plans, and professional certifications

Contact: Newton Engineering Division ↗

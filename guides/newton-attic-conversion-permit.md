# What permits and zoning rules apply to finishing an attic in Newton, MA?

> Converting an attic into living space is a building alteration that should be reviewed by Newton ISD. Newton zoning also defines a half-story by the amount of floor area with a ceiling height of at least 7 feet under a sloping roof. The project's height, floor area, and building-code compliance must be checked against the actual plans.

## Building permit and code review

- The state building code requires a permit before regulated alterations to a building. (Source: [Massachusetts State Building Code, 780 CMR, Tenth Edition, Section 105.2](https://www.mass.gov/doc/bbrs-10th-edition-building-code/download))

## Newton's half-story definition

- Newton defines a half-story under a sloping roof as a story where the area with a ceiling height of 7 feet or more is less than two-thirds of the area of the story below. (Source: [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638887019852300000))

## Questions and answers

### Does finishing an existing attic automatically count as a new full story?

Not automatically. Newton's half-story definition uses the qualifying ceiling-height area under a sloping roof. The result depends on the actual floor plan and roof section. (Source: [Newton Zoning Ordinance, Chapter 30, Definitions](https://www.newtonma.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/29823/638887019852300000))

## What to confirm with the City

- Whether the finished space meets the applicable minimum ceiling-height requirements
- Whether the floor structure can support the proposed use
- Whether stairs, guards, emergency escape, and fire protection comply
- How the work affects Newton's gross floor area and floor-area ratio
- Whether dormers or roof changes affect zoning height or setbacks
- Contact: [Inspectional Services Department](https://www.newtonma.gov/government/inspectional-services)
